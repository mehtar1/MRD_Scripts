## Therapy-induced lineage plasticity in B-ALL MRD

Minimal residual disease (MRD) following induction therapy remains a major predictor of relapse in high-risk B cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL), yet the mechanisms underlying early treatment resistance are poorly defined. Here, we generated a single-cell multiomic atlas of MRD by profiling paired diagnostic and post-induction samples from 41 pediatric patients spanning major high-risk B-ALL subtypes. By integrating single-cell transcriptomic, chromatin accessibility, and targeted DNA sequencing data, we show that early chemoresistance is not primarily driven by recurrent mutations. Instead, induction therapy is associated with increased heterogeneity of developmental arrest states and the emergence of stem- and myeloid-like leukemic blasts across subtypes. Reference mapping to a healthy pediatric bone marrow multiome combined with mutation analysis reveals that these primitive and myeloid-like cells are malignant, display distinct transcriptional and epigenetic programs, and frequently expand after treatment. Trajectory-based clustering uncovers subtype-independent patterns of arrest state remodeling linked to specific transcriptional and regulatory signatures, some of which are predictive of clinical outcome in independent cohorts. Chromatin accessibility analyses further identify myeloid-primed blast populations at diagnosis, suggesting that lineage plasticity and epigenetic priming contribute to therapy persistence. Together, our study demonstrates that early treatment resistance in B-ALL is driven by non-genetic cellular plasticity and developmental reprogramming, highlighting myeloid-like blast states as a previously underappreciated component of MRD biology that may negatively impact B-cell directed immunotherapy, but also represents a potential target for alternative therapeutic intervention.
